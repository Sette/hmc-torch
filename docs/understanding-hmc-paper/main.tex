% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
The R-matrix (ancestor closure matrix) has been shown to improve
Hierarchical Multi-Label Classification (HMC) by enforcing structural
consistency between parent and child class predictions. However, the
literature lacks a systematic understanding of \emph{where} the benefit
comes from---is it the training-time consistency loss, the inference-time
reconciliation, or both? And \emph{when} does it help---under what
hierarchy topologies, dataset sizes, and class counts?

We present a systematic decomposition of the R-matrix mechanism across
28 datasets spanning 7 domains. Our 4-way ablation isolates
training-time consistency loss from inference-time bottom-up
reconciliation, revealing that \textbf{inference-time reconciliation
accounts for the entire R-matrix benefit}---and that training-time
consistency loss actually \emph{degrades} performance by up to 0.009
F1. We then analyze scaling laws for HMC across 26 datasets, showing
that the R-matrix benefit grows with hierarchy depth and shrinks with
training samples per class, following a $\Delta\text{F1} \propto
D \cdot (N/C)^{-0.5}$ relationship. We establish reproducible baselines
with 5-seed stability ($\sigma < 0.003$ F1) across all major HMC
benchmarks.

Our findings provide the first systematic characterization of when and
why hierarchical constraints improve structured prediction, and lead
to a simple practical rule: \textbf{reconciliation always on, MC-loss
never}.

\end{abstract}
